Getting started

Flash in three steps

Use desktop Chrome or Edge with a USB data cable. Install choices come from the published release.

Downloads are resolved from the public release. Check the selected board, version and verification status; unavailable assets keep installation disabled. Historical bundled files are not a fallback. A published build does not certify every physical function.

1 · Flash the firmware

  1. Pick your board below, then plug it in with a data cable.
  2. Click Install Brucia and pick the serial port.
  3. Wait for the write to finish and the device to reboot into Brucia.

Checking published firmware assets...

Chip family: ESP32-S3

Use desktop Chrome or Edge - this browser has no Web Serial. Serial access was not allowed.

The LilyGo T-Embed CC1101 is the reference board; final hardware acceptance of current builds is pending. Other configured boards are experimental - treat it as experimental; it may not boot or some features may not work. For a normal failed firmware write, if the board will not start, hold BOOT and press RESET to re-enter download mode, then flash again.

Cannot enter download mode?
  • LilyGo T-Embed / CC1101 - hold the encoder (center) button and press RST, release RST, then release the encoder.
  • M5 Cardputer - power off, hold G0 (the top-right key), then connect USB.
  • M5 StickC / Plus - jumper GND to G0 while powering on.
  • Generic ESP32 - hold BOOT, plug in (or press RST), then release BOOT.

Source build: python3 tools/build_firmware.py -e lilygo-t-embed-cc1101. See the repository guide for uploading.

2 · Connect the app

Do this after step 1 has flashed Brucia and the device has rebooted into it. From then on you can update the firmware itself from inside the app - no need to re-flash from the browser.

  1. Install the app from Releases.
  2. Plug the device into the phone; tap Brucia on the device home.
  3. Open the app and tap Connect over USB.

The split design

What you can work with

The device captures and transmits; the phone brings compute, storage, GPS and a full GUI. Everything below is for authorized security research and education only.

Brucia's distinctive workspaces combine Android audio/DSP and SSTV, persistent Wi-Fi/BLE history and place comparisons, phone-side WPA dictionary verification, and an HTTP/HTTPS inspection proxy. De Bruijn, spam, replay and remote control are included tools, not claims of exclusive invention. Read the source-backed comparison.

01

Wi-Fi and dictionary attacks

Wardrive with phone GPS and exports; WPA/WPA2 handshake and PMKID dictionary verification on Android, with wordlists and candidate rules. Device tools include deauth, sniffers, beacon spam, monitor capture and ARP MITM.

02

MITM HTTPS proxy

Inspect HTTP and HTTPS on configured lab clients with a local CA. Clients must trust that CA; certificate pinning can prevent inspection.

03

Sub-GHz

Capture to CSV, a rolling-code-aware decoder, .sub import and export, De Bruijn brute force, a spectrum waterfall, replay, jammer and radio config.

04

NFC / RFID

PN532 tools for compatible tags: reads, NDEF writes, dump comparison and selected emulation paths. Support depends on the card type and operation.

05

Infrared and Spam IR

Remote catalog, signal learning, TV-B-Gone, raw replay and IR power spam, with one shared run across the IR screen and Spam hub. Receiver response requires a physical check.

06

Bluetooth LE

Scanner, an advertisement spam hub (Apple, Android, Windows, Samsung), a GATT explorer and BadBLE.

07

NRF24 add-on

With the 2.4 GHz module attached: channel scanner, Enhanced ShockBurst sniffer, jammer and MouseJack.

08

Tools & offload

On-device JavaScript, iButton, clock, speaker and mic, mic spectrum and ESP-NOW, plus phone-side session reports with OSINT and insights, a live console, a file manager and a remote screen mirror.

09

In-app updates

Update the app over the air and flash new device firmware over USB, straight from inside the app.

10

Audio from the mic

Driven by the ESP microphone and speaker: live spectrum, a waterfall spectrogram, an SSTV decoder and transmitter (Robot36 and more), a tone and DTMF detector/generator, and an oscilloscope.

11

Adaptive UI

The device reports module capabilities; unavailable tools are disabled. Driver initialization and a physical end-to-end test are recorded separately in the verification checklist.

12

Private and local

Everything runs on your phone and the device. No accounts, no analytics, no telemetry; nothing is collected or stored on our servers and your captures stay on your phone. Network access is used for updates, optional datasets, map tiles and services you choose, including WiGLE and network research tools.
